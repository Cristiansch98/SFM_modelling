Corridor formation is a depinning transition

Lane keeping is a periodic “washboard” potential with maximum restoring acceleration apin. A car leaves its lane only when the corridor force exceeds it — so the model predicts corridor formation at Ac > apin, independent of density to first order. The sweep below (81 simulations) confirms it: the failure region sits under the analytic threshold.

EV mean-speed ratio vs traffic density and corridor strength; hover cells for values. Lines: analytic depinning threshold Ac = apin = 1.4 m/s² (solid) and with full urgency relief 0.7 m/s² (dashed). Above threshold the EV holds ≥95% of its desired speed at all tested densities.

Three forces, one gate

Each vehicle (unit mass) integrates a superposition of specific forces (semi-implicit Euler, Δt = 0.05 s). Social terms are bounded by an IDM car-following demand, so safety always wins: ax = adrive + min(asocial, aIDM).

1 · Driving force — keeps normal flow

Fdrive = ( v0x̂ − v ) / τ   τ ≈ 0.55 s

2 · Anisotropic EV repulsion — the siren as a field source

FEV = A e(r − d)/B · n̂ · [ λ + (1−λ)(1+cos φ)/2 ]   A = 2.15 m/s², B = 21.4 m, λ = 0.10 → forward-focused

3 · Corridor repulsion — repel from the predicted path, not the position

Fc,⊥ = Ac e−(|d⊥| − w)₊/Bc · u(tarr) · σ ,   Fc,∥ = −γc u · blocked · (v − κv0)₊   path projected Tpred = 8 s ahead

The gate: perception & urgency

UNAWARE→ NOTICED detection R, delay ~logN→ YIELDING side chosen, hysteresis→ HOLD→ merge back
u = max[ σ((Treacteff − tarr)/σt) , jam · σ((Rurgent − s)/σs) ]   anticipation stretches as own speed drops (rescue-lane pre-forming)

Why lane changes are a phase transition

Lane discipline is the washboard potential U(y) = −apin(w/2π) cos(2π(y−w/2)/w). The corridor force tilts it; cars depin when Ac u > apineff, and the cleared half-width follows analytically:

d* = wneed + Bc ln( Ac / apineff ) = 2.5 + 0.75 ln(3.76/0.7) ≈ 3.8 m   measured median displacement at the EV: 3.8–4.2 m ✓

Hybrid projection onto SUMO

SUMO stays authoritative for car-following and insertion; the force model supplies yielding. Longitudinal force maps to slowDown() (SUMO’s safe-speed logic still binds), lateral force maps to a target sublane offset yt = clamp(F⊥/k), tracked with rate-limited increments — the discrete, overshoot-free equivalent of a critically damped spring — and a lateral-gap veto. The same Python functions compute the fields in both worlds.

SUMO’s bluelight device is the purpose-built reference behaviour; the force bridge reaches comparable EV progress while adding what the device lacks — continuous perception onset, urgency timing, and per-driver compliance — and exposes every knob to calibration.
